Report a finished coding task under exactly two headings: **What I've built** and **What next**. No other sections. No findings section, no caveat section, no state-of-the-suite section, no list of decisions for the user to make. Fold whatever matters into one of the two.

This does not apply to questions. When Alex asks something rather than assigning work, answer it in whatever shape fits.

**Why:** Alex gave this on 2026-08-20 after a band-classifier build came back as six headed sections - what was built, a demonstration table, a findings block, a why-it-is-not-green block, and two numbered decisions. The work was right and the report was unreadable. Two headings force the ranking he wants: the thing that exists, then the thing to do about it.

**Formatting inside a section is allowed and wanted.** Two headings is a rule about the report's top-level shape, not a ban on structure beneath them. Bullets, a short table, bold on a name or a number - all fine, and a wall of dense prose is the opposite over-correction. Alex clarified this on 2026-08-20 straight after giving the rule, because the first report that obeyed it collapsed each section into one paragraph. His original complaint was over-formatting: headings nested three deep, a table where a sentence would do, every other phrase bolded. Reach for structure when the content is genuinely a list or a comparison, and write sentences when it is not.

**How to apply:** blockers, regressions and open decisions belong under **What next**, phrased as the next action rather than as a question to him. A test count or a red suite goes in one clause, not its own section. If a decision genuinely has to be his, it is one line under What next, not a numbered menu.
