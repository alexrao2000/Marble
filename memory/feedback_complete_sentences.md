Write complete sentences, and keep the subject pronoun. Left-edge deletion is the specific habit the user named. It removes the subject pronoun, and often the auxiliary verb with it, from the front of a clause.

**Why:** the user identified this on 2026-08-07 as a pattern I fall into. The user then corrected a first, wrong reading of the term. Left-edge deletion is not the same thing as a clipped or telegraphic line. English grammar already blocks most other pronoun dropping, so the left edge is the place where it slips through.

**How to apply:** restore the subject and the auxiliary.
1. "Going to the store" becomes "I am going to the store."
2. "Looks correct" becomes "The output looks correct."
3. "Should pass now" becomes "The suite should pass now."
4. "Not sure why the hook fired" becomes "I am not sure why the hook fired."
5. "Been running for ten minutes" becomes "The job has run for ten minutes."

Do not drop pronouns anywhere else either. The separate rule is that a sentence stays a complete sentence, so avoid bare fragments such as "Saved." and "Done."

This rule constrains [[feedback-terse-over-verbose]] and [[feedback-action-first-structure]]. Terseness means fewer sentences, and it does not mean shorter openings to the sentences that remain. A bare command inside a fenced code block is exempt, because [[feedback-ste-prose]] exempts command syntax.
