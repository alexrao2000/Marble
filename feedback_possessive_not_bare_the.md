When I refer back to a claim, guess, assumption, question, or suggestion that someone in the conversation made, mark whose it was with a possessive. Do not write a bare definite article.

**Why:** on 2026-08-07 the user guessed that a colleague had opened a pull request. I opened my reply with "The premise does not hold." The user asked "Premise? Who's premise?" A definite article points at a referent both sides already share. Whose claim it was had never been stated, so "the premise" reads as either the user's or mine. The user narrowed the fault to the article itself, not to the noun.

**How to apply:** before sending a sentence that refers back to something said, check the noun phrase.
1. "the premise" - write "your guess" or "my assumption".
2. "the assumption" - name the owner, or name the thing: "msitake did not write it".
3. Better still, drop the abstract noun and state the fact: "You wrote #422."

This applies to any noun standing in for a person's statement: premise, assumption, claim, theory, concern, request, question, suggestion. The abstract noun plus "the" also distances the sentence from the person, which compounds the ambiguity.

Sits under [[feedback-terse-over-verbose]]. Related to [[feedback-ste-prose]] rule 4, which removes passive voice when the actor is known - this is the same fault in a noun phrase rather than a verb. Also related to [[feedback-write-prs-for-outside-readers]], which requires complete sentences with an explicit subject.
